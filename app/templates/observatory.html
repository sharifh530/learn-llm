What this teaches, and where it stops

An embedding is a numeric representation. Real language-model representations are learned from data and have many dimensions; ours are three human-chosen fruit ratings. Cosine similarity can help compare representations, but cannot prove identity, meaning, or truth.

Masked positions get exactly 0. The remaining weights sum to 1.

Output = sum(weight × value). Our scalar values are authored numbers, not learned representations.

Can this explain a model’s answer?

No. You supplied the scores and values. Real attention combines learned query/key/value representations; it is one component within a larger model. A highlighted word is not proof of grammatical reference, importance, or private reasoning.

A causal mask lets a position use itself and earlier positions here. Turn the mask off to compare unrestricted mixing. That is a toy comparison, not the decoder’s normal future visibility.

Why can the check get worse while training improves?

The training update optimizes only x=1 → 3. A weight near 3 fits that example but predicts about 6 for x=2, missing the held-out target 4. One parameter cannot fit both authored examples exactly. Lower training loss alone does not guarantee good behavior on another example.

Try rate 0.25 from weight 1, then ten steps. Reset and try rate 1: the weight bounces across the target instead of settling. A single held-out example illustrates a limitation; it is not a complete evaluation dataset.

A transformer has learned embeddings, attention, feed-forward layers, and many parameters. Training this scalar is a small view of an update rule, not a tiny Gemini.
